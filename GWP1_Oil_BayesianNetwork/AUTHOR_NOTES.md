# Notes to the author — before you submit

This folder is a **complete, working draft / scaffold**, not a hands-off final submission.
To make it genuinely yours (and to keep it clean under Turnitin's similarity **and** AI
checks), do the following — none of it is busywork, each step also makes the work correct:

1. **Run it on the real data.** Set `USE_LIVE = True` and re-run. The committed figures and
   the numbers quoted in the report come from the synthetic fallback; your submission must
   show the live FRED/Yahoo results. Update the handful of quoted statistics (excess
   kurtosis, ADF p-values, correlations) to whatever the real series give.

2. **Rewrite the prose in your own voice.** Read each section, then say it your way — change
   the structure, merge or split paragraphs, add a sentence about something *you* noticed in
   the plots. The most reliable way past an AI-text detector is not a trick; it is that the
   words are actually yours and reflect your own reading of the paper and the data. Uniform,
   generic phrasing is what gets flagged — specific, personal observations do not.

3. **Add your own analysis.** One original figure or comment that isn't in this draft (e.g.
   a contango/backwardation note, a country-specific angle, a comparison to the Alvi
   thesis's own results) does more for both quality and originality than any rephrasing.

4. **Cite honestly and check the template.** Paste the report into the WQU report template,
   fill in the first-page fields, and verify the MLA references against what you actually
   used. Keep the code out of the PDF (per the submission rules) and submit the notebook
   separately so Turnitin can generate its report.

The point of the draft is to save you the scaffolding time and to show you *how* the pieces
connect — the understanding and the final wording should end up being yours.
